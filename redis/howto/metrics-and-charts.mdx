The Upstash Console exposes metrics in two places: the database list, and the detail page of a single database. This page explains what each chart shows.

## Database List

The list view aggregates summary information across all of your databases.

<Frame>
  <img src="/img/metrics/databaselist.png" alt="Select a Redis database to view its metrics" width="100%" />
</Frame>

Click a database name to open its detail page. For each row the list shows:

- The region of the database
- The plan of the database (free, pay-as-you-go, fixed, or enterprise)

## Database Usage

The "Usage" tab of a database shows detailed charts. Pick the period at the top of the tab: the past hour, 3 hours, 12 hours, day, 3 days or week. Databases on a Pro plan or with the Production Pack can also select the past month. Charts refresh every minute.

### Top Commands

The ten most executed commands over the period, one line each.

### Throughput

  <img src="/img/metrics/throughput.png" alt="Redis throughput chart" width="100%" />

### Service Time Latency

## Insights

Databases on a Pro plan or with the Production Pack get additional sections on the Usage tab. Each one appears only when there is something to show.

### Overall Latency

The request latency across all commands as p50, p90 and p99.

### Where the Time Goes

Total request p99 next to the time Redis itself spent executing the command. The gap between the two is network, TLS and queueing, so this chart tells you whether a slow p99 is inside Redis or on the way to it. Two more lines explain spikes inside Redis: the time commands waited for the execution lock, typically caused by large scripts or big-key commands, and the time spent loading keys from disk.

### Keys Loaded from Disk

How many reads had to fetch their key from disk in each ten-minute window. Shown when there were any; a high count explains p99 spikes on databases that are larger than their memory.

### Where Your Traffic Comes From

- **Where requests enter today**: the regions requests arrived in and the regions that served them, with REST requests, TCP connections, bytes in and out and errors per pair. Requests served by a replica in another region than they entered are marked cross-region, and the share of bytes served cross-region is summarised at the top together with the split of commands between REST and TCP. A large cross-region share means a read region close to your clients would remove a network round trip from every request.
- **Client networks, last hour**: the networks your clients connect from, masked to /24, attributed to a cloud provider and region when the address belongs to a published AWS, Google Cloud or Cloudflare range, with each network's share of the sampled traffic and when it was last seen. A warning appears when most sampled traffic comes from other regions than the database's.
- **Requests today by client**: the SDKs, platforms and runtimes your requests come from, as reported by the Upstash SDKs with each request, and the share of requests that needed a retry. TCP clients that do not announce themselves are not counted.

### Limits and Security

Requests rejected today for exceeding the request or record size limit and commands throttled today, next to the limits of your plan; failed authentications, connections opened and keyspace notifications over the selected period; clients currently running MONITOR; bandwidth today by direction; the live data size against the monthly average; the ACL users that signed in today with their login counts; and the limits the database hit recently.

### Slow Commands

Commands that took longer than 500 milliseconds to execute today, with how often it happened and the slowest run. Only command names are recorded, not keys or arguments.

### Lua Scripts

The script latency as p50 and p99, and a table of the most executed scripts today: executions, commands they ran, failures including timeouts and memory-limit hits, average and slowest duration, the share of time spent in Lua versus in Redis commands, and memory use.

## Backup Insights

On the Backups tab, a completed backup of a Pro or Production Pack database has an **Analyze** action. It scans the backup offline, without touching the running database, and reports the number of keys, elements and bytes, the distribution of key sizes and element counts per type, the biggest keys overall and per type with their expiry, and an expiration profile that shows how much data is persistent, already expired but not yet removed, or about to expire. Once the report is ready the action turns into **Insights**.
